1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Urban tree canopies deliver critical ecological services: reducing the urban heat island effect, sequestering carbon, and managing stormwater runoff. However, urban trees face intense environmental stressors—soil compaction, restricted root zones, microclimate drought, and fungal pathogens.

Traditional municipal inspections require arborists to physically evaluate individual trees, which is subjective and unscalable. Satellite remote sensing (Sentinel-2, Landsat) provides multispectral data but at spatial resolutions (10-30m) far too coarse to resolve individual street trees. Consumer RGB cameras (smartphones, drones) have the needed spatial resolution, but lack physical Near-Infrared (NIR) sensors.

The Solution: The Urban Tree Health Monitor is an automated platform that combines deep learning object gating (YOLOv11), computer vision morphology, and peer-reviewed visible-spectrum vegetation indices (VARI, GLI) to provide accurate, explainable canopy health diagnostics from standard RGB photos.

2. System Architecture & Processing Pipeline

  • Stage 1 (Pre-Flight Validation): Rejects low-resolution (<100x100 px) or corrupted images.
  • Stage 2 (AI Gatekeeper): Employs Ultralytics YOLOv11-nano to detect and reject non-vegetation images (cars, people, urban infrastructure), preventing false positives.
  • Stage 3 (Morphological Vegetation Detector): Uses HSV color segmentation (green leaf canopy), brown tone clustering, Canny edge branching density, and vertical structuring kernels (3x25 px) to recognize woody trunks and winter trees.
  • Stage 4 (Spectral Index Engine): Computes user-selected vegetation indices (Simulated NDVI, peer-reviewed VARI, or GLI).
  • Stage 5 (Diagnostic Health Classification): Classifies canopy health into Healthy (>0.10), Moderate (-0.10 to 0.10), Stressed (≤ -0.10), or No Vegetation.
  • Stage 6 (Visualization & Alpha Blending): Renders false-color heatmaps (JET, TURBO, VIRIDIS, SUMMER) and provides an interactive opacity slider (0-100%) to overlay the heatmap directly on the real photo.
  • Stage 7 (GIS Export): Generates one-click JSON assessment reports and formatted text summaries with management recommendations.

3. Scientific Remote Sensing Formulations

Index Formula Physical Interpretation Peer-Reviewed Citation
VARI (G - R) / (G + R - B) Estimates green vegetation fraction while neutralizing lighting and atmospheric variances. Gitelson et al. (2002), Remote Sensing of Environment
GLI (2G - R - B) / (2G + R + B) Maximizes live photosynthetic tissue contrast against bare soil, mulch, and dead wood. Louhaichi et al. (2001), Int. J. Remote Sensing
Simulated NDVI (NIR_sim - R) / (NIR_sim + R) Approximates classic NIR absorption/scattering through green-dominance heuristics. Empirical Heuristic Approximation

4. Key Technical Problems Solved & Engineering Upgrades

  • Monolith Refactoring: Refactored a 2,047-line monolithic script into 5 clean utility modules (detection, vegetation, indices, visualization, export), improving maintainability and testability.
  • Classification Threshold Unification: Corrected an earlier flaw where an arbitrary 0.60 threshold was applied to raw NDVI (whose average hovers around 0.0), causing all images to be classified as 'Stressed'. Unified all thresholds to scientifically calibrated ranges (>0.10 Healthy, -0.10 to 0.10 Moderate, ≤-0.10 Stressed).
  • YOLO Graceful Fallback: Replaced hard-crash blocks with automatic graceful fallback to morphological filtering if deep learning weights are missing.
  • Interactive Alpha Blending: Built real-time OpenCV overlay blending, allowing arborists to inspect exactly which branches or leaves are diseased directly on the photo.
  • Comparative Seasonal Analysis: Added dual-image multi-temporal analysis to calculate canopy recovery deltas (Δ) between seasons or post-treatment.
  • Memory Leak Resolution: Added explicit plt.close() handlers to prevent matplotlib figure buildup in long-running Streamlit sessions.

5. Panel Defense & Viva Voce Guide (Anticipated Questions & Answers)

Q1: How can you calculate NDVI from standard RGB images without a Near-Infrared sensor?

Answer: "True NDVI physically requires a Near-Infrared sensor (700-1100 nm). In our system, Simulated NDVI serves as an empirical heuristic. However, to ensure rigorous scientific validity, we incorporated peer-reviewed visible-spectrum vegetation indices: VARI (Gitelson et al., 2002) and GLI (Louhaichi et al., 2001). These indices were specifically engineered by remote sensing researchers for RGB cameras and drone imagery, mathematically isolating chlorophyll absorption from natural sunlight."

Q2: Why not train an end-to-end deep learning classifier (e.g. CNN / ResNet) to predict Healthy vs. Stressed?

Answer: "An end-to-end CNN is a black box that outputs an opaque probability score without physical interpretability. In urban forestry, arborists require spatial precision: they need to know which specific branches are suffering chlorosis or dieback. Our hybrid architecture uses YOLOv11 for object gating and deterministic spectral indices for health mapping, yielding an explainable, pixel-level false-color heatmap and alpha overlay."

Q3: How does your algorithm avoid rejecting autumn trees or winter deciduous trees?

Answer: "Our morphological detector (utils/vegetation.py) employs a multi-stage decision pipeline. Tier 1 checks green foliage; Tier 2 inspects yellow and brown foliage in HSV space combined with Canny branch edge complexity; Tier 3 employs a directional 3x25 px rectangular kernel to identify vertical woody trunk structure. This ensures winter and autumn trees pass validation while non-vegetation images are rejected."

Q4: What is the real-world municipal and commercial impact of this project?

Answer: "Municipal urban tree inventories are currently updated only once every 5 to 10 years due to the exorbitant cost of manual arborist inspections and aerial LiDAR. With our platform, city maintenance crews or citizens with standard smartphones can perform rapid corridor health scans, download GIS-ready JSON reports, and detect drought or pathogen outbreaks months before irreversible tree death occurs."
